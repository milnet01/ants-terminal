# ANTS-3660 — `doc_dedup`: find the same passage written twice

**Status:** spec draft, cold-eyes loops 1, 2 and 4 folded (2026-07-28); the hold
was lifted by ANTS-3666, which measured the corpus so that § 1.1 is now that
run's output and § 2.3's constants are read off real numbers rather than
guessed. Loop 4 gated the ANTS-3666 seam and found two knock-ons that fold-in
had missed — `clusters[]` unrouted through the composer, and INV-9 with no test
row. The deferred MEDIUM/LOW tail is ANTS-3677.
**Kind:** implement.
**Source:** ROADMAP.md ANTS-3660 (user-request-2026-07-27).
**Blocked by:** ANTS-3664 (the finding shape). ANTS-3666 is **met** (2026-07-28).
**Blocker for:** ANTS-3663 (`doc_lint`), which composes it.
**Composes with:** ANTS-3601 (`doc_integrity`, whose verb-layer walk this
copies) and ANTS-3603 (`MarkdownScan`, for fence awareness).

**Layman:** Find the same passage written twice in two places, automatically,
instead of paying a reviewer to notice it.

---

## 1. Problem

This is the one `/cold-eyes` § 1e dimension with no mechanical check at all, and
it is the expensive one. The skill's Phase 4 states that a run which will not
converge usually has a duplication problem, and that reconciling two copies is
the *wrong* fix — they diverge again on the next edit, and later loops find them
one at a time. Its Budget section is blunter: every duplicated fact is re-read by
every lane on every loop *and* is a standing source of future findings.

**Nothing in the repo does this.** Measured 2026-07-27: `shingle` has zero hits
across `src/`. Three similarity implementations exist and none compares document
text — `SimilarCode::findSimilar` (`src/similarcode.h:74`) scores a free-text
query against *symbol signatures*; `src/remotecontrol.cpp::rcHeadlineJaccard()`
compares roadmap *headlines*;
`src/indiereviewengine.h::suggestedMerges()` runs
normalised Levenshtein over *review-lane summaries*.

### 1.1 What this corpus's duplication looks like — MEASURED (ANTS-3666)

ANTS-3666 measured `docs/` — 275 files, 105k lines — across three segmentation
rules × shingle sizes 2/3/4 × punctuation kept/stripped, 18 combinations in
19.6 s. Every number below is that run's output. Nothing here is an estimate,
and the constants in § 2.3 are read off it rather than chosen.

**The two earlier passes disagreed because they segmented differently, not
because either miscomputed.** The verb-contract pair, at 3-grams:

| Paragraph rule | Score |
|---|---|
| marker line dropped, continuations kept — this spec's original § 2.1 | **0.024** |
| list-aware: marker line joined with its continuations | **0.545** |
| list content excluded entirely | **0.000** |

Loop 1's 0.53 is the list-aware figure; loop 2's 0.038 is the original rule's.
Each pass was right about a different rule. **The original rule is the defect:**
it drops every `- **INV-N** —` marker and compares the remainder as a headless
fragment, scoring this corpus's clearest duplication at 0.024. § 2.1 now
specifies the list-aware rule, and that is a correctness fix, not a tuning
choice.

**Both pairs were also attributed to the wrong documents.** The real ones:

| Duplication | Pair | punct kept | punct stripped |
|---|---|---|---|
| verb-contract minimum | `ANTS-3662:262` ↔ `ANTS-3663:383` | 0.545 | 0.583 |
| build-cost stanza | `ANTS-3659:366` ↔ `ANTS-3664:202` | 0.306 | 0.450 |

Neither `ANTS-3660` ↔ `ANTS-3662` nor `ANTS-3660` ↔ `ANTS-3661` — the pairs the
disputed table named — reaches the top five of its own probe. Loop 2's 0.652 is
not reproducible under any of the eighteen combinations.

Four consequences, each of which changed the design:

1. **Segmentation is not a tuning parameter, and is settled here.** It moves the
   flagship pair by 23×, more than any other choice in this spec. It is fixed in
   § 2.1 and is not per-call overridable.
2. **Punctuation must be pinned, and is stripped.** Its effect is uneven and
   that is the point: stripping moves the build-cost pair 47% (0.306 → 0.450)
   and the verb-contract pair 7% (0.545 → 0.583). The larger of the two is more
   than the gap between any two candidate thresholds, so on some pairs the
   setting decides whether they are found at all. Leaving it unstated is
   precisely what let two passes compute different numbers and each conclude
   the other had erred.
3. **The earlier draft had the exclusions backwards in one direction and right
   in the other.** Right: repeated boilerplate across sibling specs *is* the
   finding, and both real pairs are 19–31 words, so a `minWords` set to suppress
   noise deletes them. Wrong: **generated artifacts must be excluded.** Dated
   `AUTOMATED_AUDIT_REPORT_*` files and the `superpowers/` plan-banner mirror
   supply roughly half of every exact-duplicate pair in the corpus while
   accounting for 79 passages — duplication that is what those files *are*.
   Excluding them drops exact duplicates from 40 to 21 (§ 2.4).
4. **Paraphrase is out of reach and the spec says so.** The four-way "Verified
   RED…" pair at ~0.17 states one idea in four rewordings and is lexically
   indistinguishable from unrelated prose on a shared subject. No shingle
   measure reaches it. § 5 states this as a limit so nobody reads a clean run as
   proof of no duplication.

## 2. Surface

### 2.1 Passages

The unit is a **paragraph**. Segmentation is stated precisely, because two
implementations that segment differently produce different findings from the
same document:

- A paragraph is a maximal run of lines that are none of: blank, a heading
  (`^#{1,6} `), a list marker (`^\s*(?:[-*+]|\d+\.)\s`), a GFM table row
  (`^\s*\|`), or masked by `MarkdownScan::fenceMask`.
- **A masked fence line acts as a blank** — it breaks a run rather than joining
  the prose around it. A code sample is not a sentence in the paragraph that
  follows it.
- **A list item is one passage: its marker line joined with the indented
  continuations beneath it**, ending at the next marker, the next unindented
  line, or a fence. § 1.1 measured the alternative — treating the marker line as
  a pure break — and it scores this corpus's clearest duplication at 0.024
  instead of 0.545, because every `- **INV-N** — …` is then compared without its
  own subject. This rule is fixed, not per-call overridable.
- Headings and table rows break a run and are not themselves paragraph content.
  A bullet list is therefore not one giant passage, which is what a naive "run
  of non-blank lines" rule would produce.

Each paragraph is normalised before comparison, in this order: strip inline-code
delimiters and markdown emphasis; **replace every non-word, non-space character
with a space**; fold all whitespace runs to one space; lowercase. Order matters —
folding before stripping would leave the delimiters adjacent to words and change
the tokens.

**Punctuation is stripped, and saying so is load-bearing.** ANTS-3666 measured
the same pairs both ways: stripping moves the build-cost stanza 47%
(0.306 → 0.450) and the verb-contract pair 7% (0.545 → 0.583). The larger is
bigger than the gap between any two thresholds this
spec might pick. Stripping is the choice that puts both § 1.1 pairs above
`minSimilarity`; keeping punctuation leaves the build-cost class unreachable at
any threshold that also yields a triageable report.

### 2.2 Shingles and the score

A paragraph becomes the set of its overlapping word `shingleSize`-grams. Two
paragraphs pair when their Jaccard similarity — `|A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|`, the measure
`SimilarCode::jaccard` (`src/similarcode.cpp:200`) already implements on a
different input — is **at or above** `minSimilarity`.

Word shingles rather than character ones, because character shingles score two
paragraphs on a shared subject as similar for sharing vocabulary. Overlapping
rather than disjoint, so a one-word insertion shifts a few shingles instead of
all of them.

### 2.3 Options, with values

Every constant has a value here. A tuning parameter named but not valued is not
a specification — the first draft named four and valued none, and its own
boundary invariant was unwritable as a result.

```cpp
namespace DocDedup {
struct Options {
    int shingleSize   = 3;      // words per shingle
    double minSimilarity = 0.40; // § 1.1 measured: catches both real pairs
                                 // (0.583 and 0.450) with punctuation stripped;
                                 // ~0.17 paraphrase is unreachable at any value
    int minWords      = 15;     // below this a pair is coincidence; the real
                                // finds are 19–31 words, so this must stay low
    int maxPassages   = 20000;  // paragraphs COMPARED; see INV-6
    int maxPostings   = 400;    // a shingle in more than this many paragraphs is
                                // a stop-shingle and is not used for candidate
                                // gathering (§ 4) — it carries no signal and is
                                // what makes the index degenerate
    // Inherited, same values as DocIntegrity::Options so the doc walks agree:
    qint64 maxDocBytes = 2 * 1024 * 1024;
    int    maxDocsPerRun = 2000;
};
}
```

`minSimilarity`, `minWords` and `shingleSize` are per-call overridable; the caps
are not, matching how `doc_integrity` treats its own.

**The calibration run happened — it is ANTS-3666, and § 1.1 is its output.**
These values are measured, not provisional. Against `docs/` at these defaults
the verb reports **276 pairs in 129 clusters**, and both § 1.1 pairs clear the
threshold (0.583 and 0.450).

**One half of the original acceptance criterion is not met, and the honest
answer is that the criterion was wrong.** It required output "small enough for a
reviewer to triage in one sitting (order tens of pairs, not hundreds)" *and* the
named pairs reported. ANTS-3666 shows these cannot both hold: the band from 0.40
to 0.75 was sampled and is **real duplication a reviewer would act on** — the
ROADMAP-flip stanza repeated across specs (0.739), two specs stating the same
`additionalProperties:false` invariant (0.625), the `join the existing
ants_core_lib` build-cost line (0.667). Raising `minSimilarity` to hit a smaller
number would discard true positives to make a report look tidy.

This spec pre-committed to that outcome — "if those cannot hold together, the
defaults are wrong" — and the defaults are not what was wrong. The criterion is
restated: **the run must report both § 1.1 pairs, and its cluster count must be
triageable in a sitting.** 129 clusters over a 105k-line corpus is the true
duplication load, and clustering is what makes it readable (§ 2.5).

### 2.4 Three exclusions, and the one that was deleted

- **Below `minWords`** — a pair of very short paragraphs shares shingles for
  trivial reasons. Kept, but set low (§ 2.3) because § 1.1 measured the real
  finds just above it.
- **Generated and templated artifacts** — paths matching
  `AUTOMATED_AUDIT_REPORT` or a `superpowers/` directory. Added on evidence,
  not taste: § 1.1 measured these 79 passages supplying roughly **half of every
  exact-duplicate pair in the corpus** — dated audit reports sharing a
  Stack Overflow link list, and the `> **For agentic workers:**` banner stamped
  into every plan file. Excluding them drops exact duplicates from 40 to 21 and
  clears the top of the report for real finds. Duplication between two
  generated files is not a defect in either; it is what generating them means.
- **Pointer lines** — a paragraph that is a single link or a `see § X`
  cross-reference. Kept, and for a reason worth stating: a pointer is the
  *shape the skill recommends* for an already-deduplicated fact ("state it once
  and make every other mention a pointer"). Reporting pointers would flag the
  fix as the defect.

**Deleted: the structural-boilerplate exclusion.** The first draft excluded
"a spec's header block, a loop-log row, a licence notice". It was defined by
three examples and no predicate, so two implementations would differ
arbitrarily — and § 1.1 showed it would delete real findings, because repeated
boilerplate across sibling specs is exactly what this corpus over-produces.
Header blocks and table rows are already excluded structurally by § 2.1's
segmentation rule; nothing further is needed.

### 2.5 Report-only, and it never picks

The verb reports pairs; **which copy is canonical it never decides.** That is
correctness, not caution: the canonical home is the section that *owns* the
fact, which is a question about document architecture, and a verb deleting the
wrong copy destroys the authoritative one. `doc_lint` (ANTS-3663) must never
auto-fix a `doc_dedup` finding.

Findings are `DocFinding::Finding` (ANTS-3664) with `verb:"doc_dedup"`,
`kind:"near_duplicate"`, `autoFixable:false`, anchored at the earlier passage.
The envelope additionally carries the structured pair —
`pairs[]: { a:{file,line}, b:{file,line}, similarity }` — because
`DocFinding::Finding` has no second-location field, so **the pair survives only
in this verb's own envelope**, not in a `doc_lint` `findings[]` row. A composing
caller that needs both ends reads `doc_lint`'s top-level `pairs[]`, which hoists
this array whole (ANTS-3663 § 2.1). There is no per-check envelope to read: that
composite returns one findings list, not N nested verb envelopes.
`file` rather than the ROADMAP bullet's `path`, matching `DocFinding::Finding`
and `DocIntegrity::Finding`; the bullet predates the shared shape.

**Pairs are also grouped into clusters, and the grouping is not cosmetic.**
N documents sharing one stanza produce N(N−1)/2 pairs but are **one** thing to
fix; reporting pairs alone makes a single repeated boilerplate line look like
forty findings. The envelope carries
`clusters[]: { passages:[{file,line}], size, maxSimilarity }` — connected
components over the reported pairs, each passage in exactly one cluster.
Measured on `docs/`: 276 pairs collapse to 129 clusters, and at tighter
thresholds the ratio widens (91 pairs → 21 clusters at 0.80). `pairs[]` is
retained unchanged, because the cluster loses which two passages actually
scored what, and INV-4 still governs it.

### 2.6 Files and hooks

Engine `src/docdedup.{h,cpp}` in `ants_core_lib`, Qt6::Core-only.

**This engine is ANTS-3664 § 2.3's stated exception to the per-document
signature**, because it compares passages *between* documents and so cannot
score any one of them alone. It accumulates, then scores once:

```cpp
// Family convention (ANTS-3664 § 2.3): text in, never opens the document.
// Unlike its siblings this returns nothing per call — a pair needs two
// documents, so scoring cannot happen until the walk is done.
void   add(const QString &text, const QString &relPath, const Options &opts = {});
Result finish();
```

`Result` carries `pairs`, `clusters`, `passagesTotal`, `passagesCompared` and
`truncated` — § 2.5's envelope, one field per JSON key. `Options` carries
§ 2.3's tuning constants plus § 2.4's excluded path globs; the caller fills it
once and passes the same value to every `add`.

Splitting `add` from `finish` is what keeps the one-open-per-document guarantee
reachable: ANTS-3663 hands each document's text to all five checkers from a
single read, and a `doc_dedup` that took a *path* would re-open every file in
the tree, defeating the composition it is being written for.

Verb layer mirrors `doc_integrity` (`src/remotecontrol.cpp::cmdDocIntegrity()`): a
`cmdDocDedup` handler plus two pure statics — `docDedupEnumerate` (the
`QDirIterator` walk, cloned from `docIntegrityEnumerate`) and
`docDedupBuildResponse`.
Registration is the set ANTS-3661 § 2.4 enumerates: one `registerToolProvider`
with `CallerCwdContract::Required`, plus the five `src/claudeintegration.cpp`
hooks. Not restated here — one canonical home, per the rule this verb exists to
enforce.

## 3. Invariants

- **INV-1** — A pair at or above `minSimilarity` reports; below it, none.
  *Test:* with `minSimilarity:0.40`, a constructed pair scoring exactly 0.40 and
  one scoring 0.39 → one pair. The boundary must be asserted from **both**
  sides: either alone passes under both `>` and `>=`, the trap ANTS-3653 INV-40
  records. Concrete values are what make this writable at all.
- **INV-2** — Fenced blocks never pair, and a masked fence line breaks a
  paragraph rather than joining it. *Test:* the same twelve-line code sample in
  two docs, nothing else shared → zero pairs; plus one doc where two otherwise
  identical prose runs are separated by a fence and another where they are not →
  the first yields two paragraphs, the second one.
- **INV-3** — All three § 2.4 exclusions hold, and structural boilerplate is *not*
  excluded. *Test:* a repeated 8-word note (under `minWords`) and the same
  `see § 4` pointer in two files → zero pairs; **plus two sibling specs sharing
  an identical 40-word header-adjacent paragraph → one pair**. The last row is
  the regression guard for § 2.4's deletion: an implementation that reinstates a
  boilerplate filter passes every other row here.
- **INV-4** — Each pair appears once, canonicalised so `a` precedes `b` in
  (file, line) order, and no paragraph pairs with itself. *Test:* three mutually
  similar paragraphs → three pairs, not six, with every `a` before its `b`.
- **INV-5** — Report-only: no finding is `autoFixable` and the engine writes
  nothing. *Test:* a run over a fixture tree → every finding
  `autoFixable:false`; plus a source-scrape of the engine for
  `QIODevice::WriteOnly` → zero hits.
- **INV-6** — `maxPassages` caps the paragraphs **compared**, not those
  enumerated: the walk still counts them all, so `passages_total` is a true
  count and `truncated:true` is set when it exceeds the cap. *Test:* a corpus of
  30 paragraphs at `maxPassages:10` → `truncated:true`,
  `passages_total:30`, `passages_compared:10`. Counting-then-comparing is what
  makes the two numbers consistent; a run that simply stopped could not report
  the first.
- **INV-7** — A shingle appearing in more than `maxPostings` paragraphs is not
  used for candidate gathering. *Test:* a corpus where one 3-gram appears in
  every paragraph and one genuine pair exists → the pair is still found, and a
  `Probe` candidate-comparison counter stays below the all-pairs count. Without
  this the index degenerates to O(p²) on exactly the boilerplate-heavy corpus
  § 1.1 measured.
- **INV-8** — The verb-contract minimum `mcp-tools.md` § Tests requires of every
  `Required` tool, in the arms ANTS-3601 INV-15 establishes. *Test:* three verb
  calls — absent `caller_cwd`, root-escaping `path`, and a well-formed
  non-existent in-root `path`.

- **INV-9** — `clusters[]` is the connected components of `pairs[]`: every
  reported passage appears in exactly one cluster, `size` equals its passage
  count, and `maxSimilarity` is the highest score among its pairs.
  *Test:* three passages pairing A–B, B–C but not A–C → **one** cluster of size
  three, not two of size two; plus two unrelated pairs → two clusters. The
  transitive row is the assertion: an implementation grouping only
  directly-scored pairs returns two clusters here and passes every other row.

## 4. RAM / build cost

**This is the one verb in the family whose cost is not linear, and it is bounded
three ways.**

A shingle set is built per paragraph and **discarded after that paragraph's
comparisons**, so peak is one paragraph's shingles plus the inverted index — not
the corpus's. The index maps shingle → paragraph ids and is what avoids the
naive all-pairs pass: a paragraph is compared only against paragraphs sharing at
least one *non-stop* shingle.

Arithmetic from the stated caps rather than an assertion: at `maxPassages` 20,000
paragraphs averaging ~60 words, each contributing ~58 3-gram shingles, the index
holds on the order of 1.2 M postings. Stored as ids against interned shingle
keys that is tens of MB, transient, dropped at end of call — no cache, nothing
held across calls. Lowering `maxPassages` is the lever if that is too much on a
constrained host.

Time is O(passages × average non-stop candidates), **and the `maxPostings`
stop-shingle prune (INV-7) is what makes that true**. Without it a shingle
occurring in a large fraction of paragraphs — and § 1.1 shows this corpus has
many — has an O(p) posting list and the run degenerates to O(p²). The prune is
named here rather than left to the implementer because an implementation without
it satisfies every other invariant and is still wrong at corpus scale.

One new translation unit in `ants_core_lib`; one new feature-test source into an
existing bundle. No new external dependency — Jaccard over `QSet<QString>` needs
nothing.

## 5. Out of scope

- **Deciding which copy is canonical, and any auto-fix** — § 2.5.
- **Semantic similarity.** Shingles find shared *words*. § 1.1's four-way pair at
  J=0.17 states one idea in four rewordings and no lexical measure reaches it;
  that is the `must_agree` class the cold-eyes fix ledger exists for. Stated so a
  clean run is never read as proof of no duplication.
- **Cross-repo comparison.** One project root, one walk.
- **Prose quality.** Two passages may be near-identical and both correct.

## 7. Open questions

- **Does it honour `doc-examples` regions (ANTS-3659)?** Two identical teaching
  examples in two specs may be genuine duplication worth surfacing, or may be
  the same illustration correctly repeated. ANTS-3659 § 2.1 makes this each
  verb's own policy and defers the answer here. **Settled by the § 2.3
  calibration run**: if marked regions dominate the output, honour the mask;
  if they contribute little, ignore it. Record the answer in § 2.4 rather than
  in a commit message. ANTS-3663 shares one walk and needs the answer before it
  ships.
- **Within-document pairs, or only across documents?** INV-4's "no self-pair"
  implies within-document pairs are legal, and a spec restating itself is a real
  finding this session produced. Provisionally **yes, both** — but it changes the
  output volume, so the calibration run reports the split and this section
  records it.
