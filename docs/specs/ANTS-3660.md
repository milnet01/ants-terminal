# ANTS-3660 — `doc_dedup`: find the same fact written twice

**Status:** spec draft (2026-07-27).
**Kind:** implement.
**Source:** ROADMAP.md ANTS-3660 (user-request-2026-07-27).
**Blocked by:** ANTS-3664 — the finding shape this emits.
**Composes with:** ANTS-3601 (`doc_integrity`, whose verb-layer walk this
copies) and ANTS-3603 (`MarkdownScan`, for fence awareness).
**Pairs with:** ANTS-3663 (`doc_lint`).

**Layman:** Find the same fact written twice in two places, automatically,
instead of paying a reviewer to notice it.

---

## 1. Problem

This is the one `/cold-eyes` § 1e dimension with no mechanical check at all, and
it is the expensive one. The skill's own Phase 4 states that a run which will
not converge usually has a duplication problem, and that reconciling two copies
is the *wrong* fix — they diverge again on the next edit, and later loops find
them one at a time. Its Budget section puts it plainly: every duplicated fact is
re-read by every lane on every loop *and* is a standing source of future
findings, so deleting one copy is cheaper in the same loop and much cheaper by
the last one.

Today each loop rediscovers them at cold-reader prices. This session is a live
example: three loops over one 500-line spec, and the fix that finally shrank the
churn was deleting seven prose copies of one argument into a single table.

**Nothing in the repo does this.** Measured 2026-07-27: `shingle` has zero hits
across `src/`. Three similarity implementations exist and none compares document
text — `SimilarCode::findSimilar` (`src/similarcode.h:74`) scores a free-text
query against *symbol signatures*; `rcHeadlineJaccard`
(`src/remotecontrol.cpp:1347`) compares roadmap *headlines*; and
`IndieReviewEngine::suggestedMerges` (`src/indiereviewengine.h:123`) runs
normalised Levenshtein over *review-lane summaries*. Prose-to-prose comparison
across a doc set is new ground.

## 2. Surface

### 2.1 Passages, not documents

The unit is a **paragraph**: a run of non-blank lines, taken from the
fence-masked document (`MarkdownScan::fenceMask`) so that two identical code
samples are never reported — an example repeated verbatim is usually correct,
and it is exactly what a docs corpus does most.

Each paragraph is normalised before comparison: fold whitespace, strip markdown
emphasis and inline-code delimiters, lowercase. Normalisation is what makes
"the cache holds **16** files" and "the cache holds `16` files" the same fact.

### 2.2 Shingles and the score

A paragraph becomes the set of its overlapping word `shingleSize`-grams. Two
paragraphs are a candidate pair when their Jaccard similarity — `|A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|`,
the same measure `SimilarCode::jaccard` (`src/similarcode.cpp:200`) already uses
on a different input — is at or above `minSimilarity`.

Word shingles rather than characters, because a character shingle scores two
paragraphs about the same subject as similar merely for sharing vocabulary; and
overlapping rather than disjoint, so a one-word insertion shifts a few shingles
instead of every one.

### 2.3 The three exclusions that make it usable, and why they are v1

Tuning risk is the whole risk here. A naive pass over this corpus reports
boilerplate at high similarity and buries the real finding. All three are part
of v1, not follow-ups:

- **A minimum passage length** (`minWords`). Short paragraphs pair at high
  Jaccard for trivial reasons — a two-sentence note and another two-sentence
  note sharing a subject can exceed any useful threshold.
- **Pointer lines are excluded.** A paragraph that is a single link, a `see § X`
  cross-reference, or a one-line stub is the *shape the skill recommends* for a
  deduplicated fact ("state it once and make every other mention a pointer").
  Reporting pointers as duplication would flag the fix as the defect.
- **Structural boilerplate is excluded** — a spec's header block, a loop-log
  row, a licence notice. These are duplicated by format, and the format is the
  reason.

### 2.4 Report-only, and it never picks

The verb reports pairs; **which copy is canonical is a judgement it never
makes.** That is not caution, it is correctness: the canonical home is usually
the section that *owns* the fact, which is a question about document
architecture, and a verb that deleted the wrong copy would destroy the
authoritative one. `doc_lint` (ANTS-3663) must therefore never auto-fix a
`doc_dedup` finding.

Findings are `DocFinding::Finding` (ANTS-3664) with `verb:"doc_dedup"`,
`kind:"near_duplicate"`, `autoFixable:false`, anchored at the *first* passage,
with the second's location and the similarity in the message. The envelope
additionally carries the structured pair:
`pairs[]: { a:{file,line}, b:{file,line}, similarity }`.

## 3. Invariants

- **INV-1** — Two paragraphs at or above `minSimilarity` are one pair; below it,
  none. *Test:* a near-identical pair differing in three words and an unrelated
  pair sharing a subject → exactly one pair, and the boundary asserted from both
  sides (a pair at exactly `minSimilarity` reports, one just under does not) —
  either alone passes under both `>` and `>=`, the trap ANTS-3653 INV-40
  records.
- **INV-2** — Fenced blocks never pair. *Test:* the same twelve-line code sample
  in two documents, nothing else shared → zero pairs. Breaks the moment the
  fence mask is dropped, and this is the population a docs corpus has most of.
- **INV-3** — All three § 2.3 exclusions hold. *Test:* a repeated two-line note
  under `minWords`, the same `see § 4` pointer in two files, and two identical
  spec header blocks → zero pairs; then the same fixture with a genuine
  duplicated paragraph added → exactly one.
- **INV-4** — Every pair is reported once, unordered, with `a` earlier in
  document order than `b`. *Test:* three mutually-similar paragraphs → three
  pairs, not six, and no self-pair.
- **INV-5** — Report-only: no finding is ever `autoFixable`, and the engine
  writes nothing. *Test:* a run over a fixture tree → every finding
  `autoFixable:false`, and a source-scrape of the engine for `QFile::WriteOnly`
  → zero hits.
- **INV-6** — Past `maxPassages` the run stops and sets `truncated:true` rather
  than silently comparing a prefix. *Test:* a corpus over the cap → flag set,
  and `passages_total` reporting the true count.
- **INV-7** — The verb-contract minimum: no `caller_cwd` →
  `caller_cwd_required`; root-escaping `path` → `bad_path`; well-formed
  non-existent in-root `path` → `ok:true`, empty findings. *Test:* three calls.

## 4. RAM / build cost

**This is the one verb in the family whose cost is not linear, and it is
bounded twice.**

A shingle set is built per paragraph and **discarded after that paragraph's
comparisons**, so peak memory is one paragraph's shingles plus the index, not
the whole corpus's. The index is the inverted map from shingle to paragraph id
— that is what avoids the naive all-pairs pass: a paragraph is only compared
against paragraphs sharing at least one shingle, which on a docs corpus is a
small minority.

Two caps, both stated because a duplication finder without them is a hazard on a
large tree: `maxPassages` (total paragraphs considered, `truncated:true` past it,
INV-6) and `maxDocBytes` per document, inherited from `DocIntegrity::Options`.
At `maxPassages` with an average paragraph of ~60 words, the inverted index is
on the order of a few MB — transient, no cache, nothing held across calls.

Time is O(paragraphs × average candidates per paragraph), not O(paragraphs²),
because of the index. Stated as a design constraint rather than an aspiration:
an implementation doing the naive nested loop satisfies every invariant above
and would still be wrong at corpus scale, which is why the index is named here
and not left to the implementer.

One new translation unit in `ants_core_lib`; one new feature-test source. No new
external dependency — Jaccard over `QSet<QString>` needs nothing.

## 5. Out of scope

- **Deciding which copy is canonical, and any auto-fix** — § 2.4.
- **Whether it honours `doc-examples` regions** (ANTS-3659). Deliberately
  undecided here: two identical teaching examples in two specs may be a genuine
  duplication worth surfacing, and the mask is shared while the policy is per
  verb (ANTS-3659 § 2.1). Settle it with a measurement against the real corpus
  during implementation, and record the answer in this spec rather than in a
  commit message.
- **Cross-repo comparison.** One project root, one walk.
- **Semantic similarity.** Shingles find the same *words*; two paragraphs
  stating one fact in wholly different language are the `must_agree` class the
  cold-eyes fix ledger exists for, and no lexical measure reaches them. Naming
  the limit so nobody reads a clean run as proof of no duplication.
